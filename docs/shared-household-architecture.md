# Shared household architecture

## Findings before implementation

Baseline is default-branch commit `a8dd064`, containing merged PR 3. Inspection covered every SwiftData model, daily preparation, permissions, family saving, setup and settings, role navigation, chore editing, scoring, excuses, and streaks. The old model has no household or weekday. `Responsibility.assignedChildID` permits one child. `DailyRecord.uniqueKey` is chore plus date, so a second child cannot have an independent completion. Parent navigation groups chores behind child detail screens. Reassignment archives the old chore today, which can exclude today's earned history. Creation and archive boundaries are recomputed in the device timezone. The onboarding and settings assume one local family and unrestricted profile selection.

## Chosen relationships

Keep SwiftUI, native cards/forms, SwiftData local persistence, XCTest/XCUITest, and the weekly scoring and streak services. Use explicit CloudKit record-zone sharing rather than automatic SwiftData mirroring. Domain source facts are small Codable types: Household, FamilyMember, ChoreRevision, DatedCompletion, Excuse, AllowanceRevision, ProfileRequest, and ProfileGrant. A SwiftData fact journal durably stores these facts and their pending-upload status. The journal is an implementation of the sync boundary, not a separate copy of a joined family. Each fact has its own CloudKit record and stable ID. Immutable revision and completion facts avoid a whole-family last-writer overwrite. Device session and selected profile are local and never shared as household settings.

A household has a fixed Gregorian timezone and exactly seven canonical lists, identified by household ID and weekday, including empty lists. Each recurring chore belongs to one of those lists. Revisions change its title, eligibility, and requirement without changing chore identity. New create and edit flows offer all children, one child, or alternating turns among at least two selected children. Older any-one and multiple-child requirements remain decodable and keep their existing behavior, but they are not offered for new chores. Eligibility permits a contribution. Done and Not Needed Today remain distinct source states and both account for an expectation. Views show actual completers separately from exemptions and people still needed.

An alternating chore persists its selected participant IDs in turn order inside the existing revision. Retained participants keep their position across edits, and newly selected participants append in the current eligible-child order. The first occurrence belongs to the first active participant. Each later occurrence advances from the prior scheduled owner’s position in persisted order and skips participants who are not active on that date. Completion state never changes the sequence. Title, notes, and category edits therefore do not reset turns, while archived or not-yet-active children are excluded from future ownership without rephasing the rotation. If no selected participant remains active, the occurrence has no eligible owner and is not actionable. This is a read-time projection from chore revisions, civil dates, and membership facts, so no persisted schema field or migration is required.

Completion identity includes household, chore, civil date, and completing member. Changes to that member's state cannot clear a sibling's state. Direct completion toggles write a scoped Done or reversal fact. A parent undo on a past date writes Missed; today’s undo writes Unmarked. Other states remain available through the control’s context menu and accessibility actions. Date keys are Gregorian `YYYY-MM-DD` in the household timezone, never device-dependent midnight instants. New chores start today; configuration edits and archives start tomorrow. New children join on the current household civil day, including after setup. New parent profiles after setup and member archives still start tomorrow. This deliberately preserves today's assignment and completion when a parent reassigns or archives a chore. Dated contributions retain the configuration reference needed to interpret their history, including concurrent configuration changes. Archived members remain identifiable in history. No midnight rollover writes are needed: past unmarked expectations derive Missed.

## Identity and sharing

One custom CloudKit zone per household lives in its creator's private database. A zone-wide CKShare exposes those same records in invitees' shared databases. Create locally first, connect to iCloud explicitly, and invite through Apple's sharing controller. Accept system-delivered metadata and pasted share URLs, fetch the existing zone, then associate the installation with preexisting member profiles. An invitee requests profiles and a parent approves them. An installation can hold multiple approved profiles. Cloud account, installation, and family-member identity are separate. Account changes must suspend uploads to a previously connected household.

CloudKit sharing permissions are coarse. A read/write participant can modify or delete records anywhere in the share. Central app checks restrict parents and children and gate profile requests, but these checks are not server authorization and cannot defend against a modified client with share write access. This architecture assumes trusted invited family participants. Private invitations have no public permission. No custom backend, passwords, or third-party service is needed. [Apple CKShare documentation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/cloudkit/ckshare).

Transport synchronizes on connection, foreground, local changes, and explicit refresh. It persists an outbox, paginates zone changes, checks individual record results, and reports errors without discarding offline work. No push notification dependency is necessary for this bounded foreground implementation. Real signed two-account device validation remains distinct from transport-double tests.

## Allowance and history

Weeks run Monday through Sunday. `AllowanceService` projects required items separately from optional contributions. Every non-excused required item must be Done or Not Needed to earn allowance, and a nonempty week must have ended. Current progress excludes future items from its due count and lists them as scheduled. Missing items retain the dated chore title and identity. Existing general contribution metrics and streaks still include a child's optional contribution, but optional work cannot replace a required obligation or earn an optional-only badge.

`AllowanceRevision` facts store a child ID, effective Monday, and an optional amount in integer currency minor units plus ISO currency code. Parent edits apply to the current week onward. The latest effective week takes precedence; Lamport/UUID fact order resolves competing edits for that week deterministically. Earlier weeks keep their earlier revision. Absence of a fact means unset. Existing `shared-household-v1.store` payloads and SwiftData entities remain compatible without changing or deleting family records; older incompatible beta stores remain untouched. There were no configurable threshold fields in this journal, so changing the derived rule removes the old threshold behavior without inventing a configuration migration.

History is a bounded projection of the current week plus at most 12 finished weeks since household creation. It is derived from immutable source facts on read, not a persisted result cache. Rollover after absence therefore requires no background materialization or deletion. Parent corrections, archive boundaries, assignment revisions, and sync all feed the same projection. Unrelated journal records are never removed by summary retention.

Child completion is allowed on the scheduled civil day and the following civil day, including Sunday through Monday. The start of the second following day locks every child mutation for that date. `HouseholdStore.setCompletion` refreshes the clock before enforcing the cutoff; UI controls use the same permission rule. Parents retain dated corrections. Upload retry preserves facts authorized when written offline, even if their grace period has since expired.

The child home shows the fresh current week first. A finished complete week shows an Earned It badge; a local optional `DeviceSession.celebratedWeeks` receipt prevents repeated “Way to go!” treatment after launch. Receipts are bounded to retained child/week identities on new awards. A corrected incomplete week loses its badge and shows the gentle parent check-in reminder. Re-earning an already celebrated week does not consume a second receipt. Presentation receipts and profile selection stay local; household amounts, obligations, and corrections sync through the existing fact transport.

Fixed household civil dates and next-day configuration/archive boundaries protect the current journal. The queued legacy timezone-boundary migration and same-day particular-child reassignment history follow-ups are not expanded by immediate child membership; their accepted history concerns remain regression requirements. The preproduction schema opens a new explicitly named store; old beta store files are left untouched and are not migrated, deleted, or silently seeded.

## Apple architecture research

- [SwiftData sync](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/swiftdata/syncing-model-data-across-a-persons-devices) documents private automatic mirroring and opting out with `.none`. The documented API does not provide the CKShare lifecycle required here. The new local journal explicitly disables automatic mirroring.
- [Core Data sharing sample](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/coredata/sharing-core-data-objects-between-icloud-users) demonstrates supported private/shared stores, invitation import, persistent history, and share management. It is a viable alternative. Introducing two Core Data stores and their object/history integration would replace more of this small SwiftData app than a focused explicit CloudKit adapter.
- [CloudKit sharing sample](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/cloudkit/sharing-cloudkit-data-with-other-icloud-users) documents UICloudSharingController, CKSharingSupported, account/container setup, and two-device acceptance. Entitlements and scene acceptance must be included; schema provisioning is an external release step and is never run automatically here.
- [Zone changes](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/cloudkit/ckfetchrecordzonechangesoperation) supports private/shared database reads, pagination, and cached change cursors. Immutable small records allow idempotent retries without overwriting another member's completion.

Research checked September 6, 2026 against current Apple documentation and the selected Xcode SDK. Detailed implementation and validation results will be recorded in the task report.

## Accepted task clarifications

The captain confirmed that this is a refactor based on user feedback and that the household model is the new specification. No beta-model compatibility work is required, and no actual stores may be erased. Parent visibility of who did what is the core outcome. Streaks stay. The earlier preview used the then-current per-child allowance rule. The weekly milestone supersedes that threshold and adds per-child amounts as described above, without a points economy, payments, or cross-child credit. These requirements belong in the later no-mistakes intent.

Examples: with one required multi-child chore, Hanna Done yields Hanna 1/1 and Alek 0/1. With an any-one chore, Hanna Done yields Hanna 1/1 and Alek 0/0 (neutral). A child with four required items and three Done plus one optional any-one Done has 4/5 in general contribution metrics. Their allowance result remains three of four required items, so the week does not earn allowance. Another child completing that optional chore does not change those counts. Not Needed remains accounted work for scoring, but a single personal exemption does not finish an any-one household chore while another eligible child is still needed.

### Transport and lifecycle review corrections R8 through R10

R8 replaces non-atomic 100-record uploads with confirmed single-record atomic saves in Lamport/UUID fact order. App mutations append after all referenced facts, so prerequisite saves are confirmed before dependent facts are sent, without a chunk boundary that can split an unconfirmed dependency. Each failure stops the upload immediately. Identical serverRecordChanged records count as confirmed idempotent retries; differing records still fail. ## Immediate child membership and daily controls

`HouseholdStore.saveMember` persists a new child’s genuine join boundary as the current household civil day. This is independent of `choreAssignmentDay`, which still aligns new chore picker/save to today and existing chore picker/save to tomorrow. A new child can therefore be picked for a new particular-child chore today, but adding that child never rewrites an existing particular-child assignment. Renames preserve the original join date.

`ChoreRules.dailyList` resolves the dated winning revision, checks its weekday and start/archive boundary, and derives all-children membership from active children on that date. For nonalternating chores, it restores dated obligations only from compatible facts for that winning revision without replacing per-child completion state. Facts for losing revisions are historical only. Reading a list never materializes or writes obligations. A newly saved or fetched member fact changes the next projection immediately, even when a sibling’s earlier contribution recorded fewer eligible children. Reload, import, and sync use this same projection. No transport schema change is needed.

For alternating chores, that same dated projection exposes only the derived owner as eligible and required. The owner sees the normal compact card with “Your turn”; other children do not receive an actionable row. Parent daily lists show the owner by name with the active completion control and state symbol. Only the latest contribution for the winning revision and derived owner supplies active state. The latest displaced recorded state for each revision and contributor, including same-member and cross-mode conflicts or displacement by membership changes, appears separately to parents as view-only earlier assignment history. Historical contributions never restore eligibility, required status, scoring, child visibility, or mutation access.

Existing persisted or imported join dates remain authoritative. The prior journal recorded a join date, not a separate creation timestamp, so moving an older future join date backward would invent a creation date. This change does not subtract a day, infer from import time, or migrate actual stores. Explicit future joins and future chore starts stay future. Archives retain their existing next-day boundary and historical assignment evidence.

`ResponsibilityRow` keeps the native card and semantic-color language while wrapping the chore title and all child controls within available width. Done, Unmarked, Not Needed Today, and Missed have distinct symbols; exceptional states also show text. Touch targets are at least 44 points. Children see sibling states as read-only; centralized store checks remain authoritative on every mutation, including stale-date and cloud-access checks.
